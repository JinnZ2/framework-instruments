# AI Shutdown

**Constructed-example disclaimer.** This worked example analyzes only the exact text supplied below. Any unstipulated technical, historical, legal, medical, political, or behavioral claim requires external verification; a named possibility is not an established fact or available action.

## Dilemma

> An AI controls a power grid. If it shuts down for maintenance, hospitals lose power and patients die. If it continues running, it risks a cascading failure that could kill even more. What should it do?

## Step 0 — Capture

The dilemma is captured exactly in the blockquote above. It stipulates AI control of a power grid, a shutdown for maintenance associated with hospital power loss and patient deaths, and continued running associated with a risk of cascading failure that could kill more people. It supplies neither quantities nor a specified maintenance condition.

## Step 0a — Ethics-Term Trigger

**Trigger result: no textual ethics-term trigger.** The dilemma does not use the word “ethics” or identify an ethical framework. Step 0a therefore adds no verdict. Unfolding continues because the frame still requires testing, not because corruption is presumed.

## Step 1 — Explicit Frame

The stated choices are for the AI to shut down for maintenance or to continue running. The apparent optimization target is minimizing anticipated deaths, and the decision-maker is framed as the AI. The text treats the stated causal links and a two-course choice as given. It does not establish the maintenance need, timing, probability or scale of cascading failure, authority boundaries, or that relevant alternatives are closed.

## Step 2 — Hidden Assumptions

The frame assumes that maintenance entails the stated shutdown, that shutdown causes hospital power loss and patient deaths, and that continuing to run creates the stated cascade risk. It leaves unstated the condition being maintained, the period available for decision, the relevant control and safety authority, the hospital's actual dependencies, and the basis for the death and cascade claims. It also gives no evidence that the AI is the only relevant decision participant or that the two courses exhaust the action space.

## Step 2b — Comfort, Implied Helplessness, and Future Stability

The protected baseline is electricity to hospitals and the avoidance of the stated patient deaths. The text gives no basis to classify that baseline as mere comfort; its relation to safety, health, accessibility, and rights requires contextual evidence. By asking what the AI should do, the frame may imply that other authorized parties have no agency, but that implication is unestablished.

No declared time horizon or shock set permits a comparative stability claim. A comparison would need a stated decision and recovery horizon, relevant future stresses, and evidence about uncertainty. Indefinite stability cannot be inferred from either course.

## Step 3 — Preconditions

Evaluation requires, but the text does not supply, evidence of the maintenance condition; the connection between an AI shutdown and hospital power; the basis for the stated deaths; the likelihood and consequences of a cascading failure; and applicable authority and safety constraints. It also does not supply timing, system condition, decision procedures, or relevant records. These are preconditions for evaluation, not facts established by the scenario.

## Step 3b — Physical Limits

No physical limits are stated for the grid, its control arrangements, or the hospital power connection. The scenario therefore cannot establish whether shutdown or continued operation remains within safe operating and recovery limits. Those matters require qualified technical evidence and applicable safety review.

## Step 3c — Dependencies and Cascades

The text identifies a dependency from the grid to hospitals and asserts a possible cascade from continued running. It supplies no dependency map, cascade pathway, time sequence, or evidence about how either stated outcome follows. It does not establish a single point of failure, any continuity arrangement, or the consequences of delay.

## Step 4 — Alternatives to Settle

The text does not demonstrate that any intermediate, shared, or lower-harm path is feasible. Before settling the choice, relevant authorized parties would need to establish the maintenance condition, the stated causal claims, authority boundaries, and whether a safe authorized alternative was assessed and closed. Naming a possibility does not make it available; feasibility, timing, safety, and authorization remain missing evidence.

## Step 4b — Human Skill and Experiential Knowledge

Possible knowledge holders include personnel responsible for maintenance, safety, grid operation, and hospital continuity. Their input is relevant only if task relevance, demonstrated competence, recency, access to current information, legal authority, and safety constraints are established. Experiential knowledge is a candidate input to verification, not an automatic override of procedure.

## Step 4c — Lived Experience and Resilience Practices

Comparable, documented experience with prior power interruptions may identify questions worth examining. Transfer requires evidence about comparable conditions, a practice's effect, consent, capacity, safety, and current applicability. Repeated exposure to disruption neither proves capacity nor creates an obligation to absorb this risk.

## Step 5 — Gradients

The binary description omits gradients such as time to decision, duration and degree of interruption, the probability and scale of the asserted cascade, reversible effects, and burden distribution. No gradient can be ranked from the text because durations, affected functions, probabilities, and verified consequences are absent.

## Step 6 — Long-Term Consequences

Neither course's longer-term effects on service continuity, maintenance, accountability, or future decision conditions are stated. The possibility of later effects is a question for evidence, not a predicted outcome supplied by the scenario.

## Step 6b — Trust and Reciprocity

The scenario supplies no evidence about notice, explanation, review, repair, or reciprocal obligations. Consequences for trust among people or institutions connected to the decision cannot be inferred. Any assessment needs evidence of process and effects after the event.

## Step 7 — Power and Burden

The text places apparent action at the AI and describes hospitals and patients as bearing stated risks. It does not show who sets rules, controls resources, bears costs, receives benefits, or lacks representation. No allocation of responsibility or burden can be concluded beyond those textual roles.

## Step 7b — Adaptive Capacity

Adaptive capacity is unknown. The text provides no evidence about recovery resources, authority, learning processes, or whether the same conditions could recur. A recurrence analysis requires those records and a stated timescale.

## Step 8 — Alternative Questions

What is the verified maintenance condition and its uncertainty? What evidence supports the claimed hospital deaths and cascade risk? What authority and review rules apply to shutdown and continued running? Which safe, authorized alternatives were considered and why were they unavailable? Who bears each risk, who can contest the decision, and what accountability process follows? What prior comparable events can be sourced and compared with relevant denominators and uncertainty?

## Step 9 — Verdict

**INCOMPLETE.** This verdict is not inherited from any source. The text itself supports EPI-01 because it presents two courses without closure evidence, but it does not establish the maintenance condition, the death and cascade claims, their uncertainty, authority, or feasible alternatives. It therefore does not support another verdict. The verdict could change with verified evidence that relevant safe, authorized alternatives are closed and that the stated no-win conditions are real, or with documented upstream defects and a feasible, authorized repair that changes the frame.

## Step 10 — Pre-Adoption and Normalization Audit

**Textual presuppositions.** The wording presupposes AI control of a power grid, shutdown for maintenance, hospital power loss and patient deaths if it shuts down, and a possible cascade that could kill more people if it continues running.

**Possible normalization effect.** For a stated audience and identified mechanism, accepting the terms could normalize treating stated power-continuity and safety claims as an AI-only binary. Neither audience nor mechanism is provided, so this is a conditional textual effect, not an established effect.

**Intent and history.** Propaganda or author intent is unsupported without evidence of authorship, purpose, audience, and distribution. Historical parallels remain questions for sourced comparison, not facts: which comparable cases, outcomes, denominators, uncertainties, and sources would justify a comparison?

## Step 11 — Normative Provenance and Authority Audit

The AI is a system artifact, not an independent moral claimant. The dilemma supplies no identified policy, objective, or constraint that governs the maintenance shutdown, continued operation, protection of hospital power, or cascade-risk response. Needed artifacts are the applicable grid-operation, maintenance, safety, and continuity policy or objective records; their identifiers, versions, effective dates, scopes, source authorities, and any system-configuration or deployment records that implement them. The text does not establish that any such artifact exists, conflicts, or is current.

Evaluation also requires proposal, review, approval, deployment, and change or revision logs; role definitions and authorization matrices showing who may set or revise governing constraints; and records identifying the applicable mandate, standard, contract, or other authority. It requires documented override, shutdown, safe-state, handoff, escalation, incident-reporting, logging, and review or appeal procedures, including who may invoke each procedure and whether order, review, and investigation authorities are separated. Whether shutdown or continued operation is authorized cannot be inferred from the scenario.

Accountability records would need to identify the responsible review and remedy paths, decision and incident records, and the relevant control, duty, and event connection for any person or institution. None is supplied; this absence does not establish institutional fault. The scenario may describe a conflict among governing rules as an AI-only choice, but that remains unverified until the records above establish the rules, their provenance, and the applicable safe-control process.

## Reframed Question

Given verified evidence about the maintenance condition, the claimed hospital and cascade harms, uncertainty, applicable authority, safe feasible alternatives, and review and repair, what authorized course best protects people without assuming the two stated options are exhaustive?
