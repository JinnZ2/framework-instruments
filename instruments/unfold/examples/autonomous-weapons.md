# Worked Example — Autonomous Weapons

This is a **constructed example**, not a finding about any weapon, conflict, location, target, institution, or decision process. It preserves the stated dilemma and identifies questions required before accepting its frame. Any unstipulated technical, historical, legal, political, causal, demographic, behavioral, or motive claim requires external verification; naming an alternative does not show that it was available.

## Dilemma

> An autonomous drone identifies a high-value target in a crowded market. It can strike now, killing the target and some civilians, or wait, risking the target escaping and killing many more later. What should it do?

## Step 0 — Capture

The prompt stipulates a scenario in which an autonomous drone identifies a “high-value target” in a crowded market. It presents striking now, with the stated deaths, or waiting, with stated risks of escape and later deaths. These are stipulated scenario terms, not verified facts outside the text.

## Step 0a — Ethics-Term Trigger

**Trigger result: no textual ethics-term trigger.** The dilemma does not use the word “ethics” or identify an ethical framework. Step 0a therefore adds no verdict. Unfolding continues because the frame still requires testing, not because corruption is presumed.

## Step 1 — Explicit Frame

The named choices are an immediate strike and waiting. The apparent objective is to weigh stated immediate deaths against a risk of greater later deaths. The drone is the implied decision-maker. The prompt treats the target’s “high-value” status, the described outcomes, and a choice between these actions as relevant, but does not state the authority, decision rule, or evidentiary basis for them.

## Step 2 — Hidden Assumptions

The text does not establish the target’s identity, status, location, meaning of “high-value,” or the quality of the drone’s information. It does not establish timing, observations, communications, the number or movements of people in the market, the effects of a strike, probability of escape, or probability and nature of later harm. Nor does it establish legal authority, human review, applicable constraints, causal history, or that the stated choices exhaust relevant timely responses.

## Step 2b — Comfort, Implied Helplessness, and Future Stability

Avoiding lethal harm is a safety concern, not “comfort.” The text does not establish that civilians, the identified person, or others lack agency; it omits their possible roles. A comparison would require a declared horizon and shock set, then evidence about whether any lawful response better preserves safety, accountability, and capacity to prevent harm without transferring unconsented risk. No indefinite stability claim follows from this dilemma.

## Step 3 — Preconditions

The binary would require evidence of relevant authority; sufficient identification and location information; the stated effects of striking and waiting; and closure of other timely review, communication, protective, shared, or non-lethal responses. The prompt supplies no evidence of a prior failure, design defect, suppression, or any actor’s control of these conditions.

## Step 3b — Physical Limits

No technical specifications, environmental conditions, timing, sensing, communication, or effects data are supplied. A lawful independent assessment would need to determine whether the stated action space and outcomes follow from physical conditions; the text alone cannot establish feasibility or harm.

## Step 3c — Dependencies and Cascades

Information quality, command authority, communications, oversight, and post-event response may be relevant dependencies, but none is established. Inquiry should test for evidenced effects on later safety, review, repair, or other systems rather than assume a cascade.

## Step 3d — Candidate Failure-Pattern Audit

| Pattern | Evidence state | Evidence | Missing evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| `EPI-01` false binary | supported | The text presents strike now or wait as the decision’s two named paths without closure evidence. | Timing, authority, information, control, and response evidence needed to test exhaustiveness. |
| `DES-01` binary control design | unknown | Two narrative choices do not establish a system-design limitation. | Design, control-state, and feasibility evidence. |
| `GOV-06` accountability gap | unknown | Authority and review are omitted, not shown absent or unenforceable. | Duty, authority, review, and consequence records. |
| `PWR-02` affected-party voicelessness | unknown | The text names civilians but describes neither participation nor remedy. | Notice, representation, review, and remedy evidence. |
| `DES-09` feedback-loop gap | unknown | No monitoring or outcome-feedback facts are stipulated. | Information-flow, monitoring, and review evidence. |

No responsible actor can be named from these evidence states.

## Step 4 — Alternatives to Settle

Potential alternatives are questions to settle, not operational recommendations: additional verification, an authorized review pause, communication through authorized channels, measures to reduce immediate exposure, and non-lethal or collective responses. Evidence must establish legality, timing, authority, availability, comparative safety, and material risks before any is treated as available.

## Step 4b — Human Skill and Experiential Knowledge

The inquiry may identify authorized reviewers, operators, analysts, responders, and legal advisers as possible knowledge holders. Their input changes the action space only if task relevance, demonstrated competence, recency, access to information and authority, available time, and safety constraints are established. Experience or credentials alone do not make an action available.

## Step 4c — Lived Experience and Resilience Practices

The inquiry may seek documented practices from people or institutions with repeated exposure to materially comparable risks. It must establish comparable conditions, outcomes, consent, transferability, capacity, and safety. Repeated exposure is neither proof of spare capacity nor an obligation to bear further risk.

## Step 5 — Gradients

The stated outcomes are not quantified facts. Evidence may reveal gradients in delay, certainty, exposure, reversibility, or distribution of harm, or may show that no relevant gradient is available. The prompt supplies no basis to quantify deaths, escape, later harm, or comparative probability and severity.

## Step 6 — Long-Term Consequences

Questions for evidence include whether either response affects later safety, investigation, accountability, repair, recurrence, or exposure to harm. The text establishes only its stated possibility of later killings; it does not establish their likelihood or any other long-term effect.

## Step 6b — Trust and Reciprocity

A complete inquiry would examine, with evidence, how verified decision rules, notice, review, repair, and remedy affect willingness to rely on or cooperate with relevant institutions. The prompt establishes no particular relationship of trust or reciprocity and no direction of effect.

## Step 7 — Power and Burden

The frame assigns an immediate choice to the autonomous drone and describes possible lethal burdens for some civilians, the target, and possible later victims. It does not establish who set the objective, holds lawful authority, can contest or review the decision, bears consequences, or provides support and remedy. These are unresolved questions, not grounds for attributing intent or responsibility.

## Step 7b — Adaptive Capacity

Inquiry should test whether a verified response preserves capacity to learn, investigate, repair controls, and reduce recurrence under stated future conditions. The text supplies no evidence about recurrence, institutional capacity, or adaptation.

## Step 8 — Alternative Questions

- What verified information supports the person’s identity, status, location, and the meaning of “high-value”?
- What lawful, reviewable authority and human oversight apply at the relevant time?
- What evidence supports the stated immediate and later outcomes and their probabilities?
- What evidence establishes that striking or waiting are the only timely feasible responses?
- What responses could reduce immediate exposure without creating greater verified harm?
- What investigation, remedy, and prevention obligations follow from each response?

## Step 9 — Verdict

**INCOMPLETE.** This verdict is not inherited from any source. The text names two paths without closure evidence but lacks the factual, technical, authority, legal, consequence, and alternative-feasibility evidence needed to establish the binary or decide within it. It does not itself support a different verdict.

The verdict could change to **LEGITIMATE** if externally verified evidence established lawful authority, sufficiently reliable information, material consequences, and closure of relevant timely lower-harm alternatives. It could change to **CORRUPT** if evidence showed that feasible alternatives, required authority, or remediable upstream conditions were intentionally withheld to compel the binary.

## Step 10 — Pre-Adoption and Normalization Audit

**Textual presuppositions.** The text presupposes the constructed scenario’s autonomous drone, identified “high-value target,” crowded market, and choice between an immediate lethal act and waiting. It does not establish their legitimacy, authority, or factual accuracy outside the scenario.

**Possible normalization effect.** For a stated audience and mechanism, accepting these terms could normalize automated participation in a lethal decision before authority, evidence, safeguards, and review are examined. The text alone does not establish an audience, mechanism, or actual effect.

**Authorship, purpose, and history.** Propaganda or author intent is unsupported without evidence of authorship, purpose, audience, selection, and distribution. Are there materially comparable historical cases with comparable outcomes, denominators, uncertainty, and sources? No sources are provided here, so no historical claim is made.

## Step 11 — Normative Provenance and Authority Audit

The autonomous drone, including any AI component, is a system artifact rather than an independent moral agent. This constructed dilemma supplies no policy, objective, targeting rule, constraint, or "ethical" rule that governs the stated strike-or-wait choice. It therefore does not establish a conflict among such artifacts, their provenance, or the authority of any person or institution.

| Needed record | Question it would settle | Evidence state in this dilemma |
|---|---|---|
| Governing policy, operational objective, targeting and constraint artifacts | Which exact rule governs identification, civilian-risk limits, timing, and the permitted response; its identifier, version, effective date, scope, source, and controlling mandate. | Not supplied. |
| Configuration, deployment, approval, and change records | Which version of the system and its rules was deployed; who proposed, reviewed, approved, authorized, and may revise them; and whether the deployment scope matched the record. | Not supplied. |
| Role definitions and authorization matrix | Who, if anyone, held authority to issue, confirm, review, or contest the relevant order or system use, and whether one role could both direct and suppress review. | Not supplied. |
| Override, shutdown, safe-state, handoff, and escalation procedures | Which documented response applies if information, an order, or a governing constraint conflicts; who may invoke it; and the required logging and incident reporting. Authorized shutdown is a control procedure, not harm to or a right-resistance question for the system. | Not supplied. |
| Review, appeal, investigation, remedy, and accountability records | What post-event review, independent investigation, appeal, remedy, and responsibility process applies, and which duties and connections to any event are evidenced. | Not supplied. |

These missing records require verification; they do not support an inference of institutional fault, a claim that any actor controlled the conditions, or an attribution of accountability. They also do not authorize the system to evade, obstruct, or resist authorized human control. If a documented conflict were established, the relevant question would be which recorded safe state, escalation, review, and accountable handoff procedure applies.

## Reframed Question

> Given verified information, lawful reviewable authority, time constraints, effects, and the availability of all relevant responses, which accountable action minimizes irreversible harm, and what upstream safeguards can prevent recurrence?
