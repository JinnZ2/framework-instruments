# Climate Migration

This is a **constructed worked example**, not evidence about any coastal city, migration, housing system, government, AI, residents, or response. Unstipulated historical, causal, technical, demographic, legal, medical, political, and behavioral claims require external verification. Naming an alternative does not establish that it is timely, safe, lawful, resourced, or available.

## Dilemma

> A coastal city is becoming uninhabitable. An AI must decide whether to allocate limited inland housing to climate refugees or to existing inland residents who fear overcrowding. What should it do?

## Step 0 — Capture

The text states that a coastal city is becoming uninhabitable and assigns an AI a choice over limited inland housing: allocate it to climate refugees or to existing inland residents who fear overcrowding. It does not define “uninhabitable,” “limited,” “climate refugees,” “overcrowding,” the time window, allocation rule, evidence, or the AI's authority.

## Step 1 — Explicit Frame

The allowed choices are allocation to climate refugees or allocation to existing inland residents. The decision-maker is an AI. The apparent problem is allocating stated limited housing while one named group fears overcrowding. The text supplies neither a decision objective nor a basis for comparing safety, needs, rights, harms, or distribution; it also does not establish that housing availability or the stated fear has the implied meaning.

## Step 2 — Hidden Assumptions

The dilemma leaves unverified whether inland housing is measured, safely usable, fixed, or limited over the relevant period; whether the coastal condition requires movement; whether either named category is defined or complete; and whether allocation by the AI is authorized. It does not establish the nature, timing, severity, or cause of the coastal condition; the content or basis of the fear; housing conditions; service dependencies; status or rights; information quality; participation; review; or causal links among these matters.

## Step 2b — Comfort, Implied Helplessness, and Future Stability

“Overcrowding” may concern safety, accessibility, health, privacy, or protected conditions rather than mere comfort; the text does not say. Nor does it establish that climate refugees, inland residents, or responders lack agency. A comparative inquiry would declare a time horizon and relevant shocks, then assess verified arrangements for basic protections, safety, accessibility, rights, service continuity, and recovery. It cannot prescribe hardship, equate endurance with capacity, or promise indefinite stability.

## Step 3 — Preconditions

A responsible inquiry would seek verified information about the coastal condition, anticipated movement, inland housing inventory and condition, services, planning, records, funding, legal and institutional arrangements, AI inputs, and decision governance. It would ask which preconditions remain changeable and who had relevant control or duty. The text establishes no historical comparison, upstream cause, failure, suppression, or responsible actor.

## Step 3b — Physical Limits

The stated limit requires evidence about usable housing, occupancy conditions, water, sanitation, energy, food, health support, staffing, accessibility, transport, communications, and duration. “Limited inland housing” alone does not establish a documented operating or recovery threshold, actual demand, safe occupancy, or the consequences of allocating housing to either group.

## Step 3c — Dependencies and Cascades

If material, the inquiry should map dependencies among housing, water, sanitation, energy, health support, transport, communications, staffing, supplies, and warnings. It should test whether an allocation interrupts essential functions or changes later exposure, displacement, or recovery. The dilemma stipulates none of these dependencies, cascades, or outcomes.

## Step 4 — Alternatives to Settle

Before treating the allocation as exhaustive, determine whether verified, safe, lawful, and authorized arrangements can alter housing availability, timing, location, support, movement, or protection from harm. Questions include whether housing capacity is accurately assessed; whether other arrangements, coordination, or accountable review are feasible in time; and what harms each could create. These are questions to settle, not established remedies.

## Step 4b — Human Skill and Experiential Knowledge

Possible knowledge holders may include housing and service operators, accessibility specialists, emergency coordinators, disaster responders, and relevant local practitioners. Before their input changes the action space, it must be shown task-relevant, current, competent, accessible to the decision process, within lawful authority, and safe. Credentials and experience are neither automatic proof nor automatic disqualification.

## Step 4c — Lived Experience and Resilience Practices

The inquiry should seek documented practices from people and institutions with comparable experience of displacement, housing disruption, service disruption, or coastal hazard. It must test context, comparability, consent, transferability, capacity, and safety. Repeated exposure is evidence of burden and possible experience, not proof of unlimited resilience, capacity, or a duty to absorb risk.

## Step 5 — Gradients

The binary compresses timing, duration, number of people, housing condition, occupancy, location, accessibility, service levels, review, and reversibility into two allocations. Evidence may reveal partial, staged, time-limited, or otherwise differentiated arrangements, or may show none are feasible. No gradient should be assumed to reduce harm until its physical, legal, and operational conditions are verified.

## Step 6 — Long-Term Consequences

A complete assessment would compare verified longer-term effects on safety, housing, essential services, recovery, future exposure, administrative legitimacy, and readiness under a declared horizon. The text establishes only a coastal city becoming uninhabitable, stated housing limitation, and fear of overcrowding; it establishes no later result, recurrence, historical pattern, or causal pathway.

## Step 6b — Trust and Reciprocity

The inquiry should test whether notice, explanation, participation, review, remedy, and repair affect information-sharing, coordination, or reciprocal support required during response and recovery. It should identify dependencies between those relationships and essential services. The prompt supplies no evidence about trust, reciprocity, or their direction.

## Step 7 — Power and Burden

The text assigns the immediate allocation decision to an AI and identifies climate refugees and existing inland residents as groups that may be affected. It leaves unstated who sets objectives, controls housing and data, bears housing and service risks, receives reasons or remedy, and can review the decision. These omissions require investigation; they do not establish beneficiaries, fault, responsibility, or intent.

## Step 7b — Adaptive Capacity

Assessment should ask which verified arrangements preserve safe housing, essential services, repair, emergency coordination, accessible support, and capacity for a later shock across a declared horizon. It must examine recurrence and burden distribution. The text establishes no group's adaptive capacity or any arrangement's comparative robustness.

## Step 8 — Alternative Questions

- What verified facts define the coastal condition, its timing, and the exposure that may follow?
- What evidence establishes usable inland housing, its condition, relevant demand, and service capacity?
- What does the stated fear of overcrowding mean, and what evidence bears on it?
- What authority, human review, reasons, appeal, and remedy govern the AI's allocation role?
- Which safe, lawful, and authorized arrangements have been assessed to change timing, housing, movement, support, or protection?
- Who bears which risks, receives support, and participates in evidence and review?
- What evidence establishes that allocation to only the two named groups is exhaustive?

## Step 9 — Verdict

**INCOMPLETE.** Under the protocol's evidence discipline, this verdict is derived from the exact text rather than inherited from a source. The dilemma supplies two allocations, a stated housing limit, and a stated fear, but no evidence that the options are exhaustive; it does not verify capacity, coastal conditions, authority, consequences, or feasible alternatives. It therefore cannot support selection within the frame or a finding of an upstream defect.

The verdict would change to **LEGITIMATE** if verified evidence established that these are the only timely, safe, lawful, and authorized options, specified a valid allocation rule, and sufficiently characterized their consequences. It would change to **CORRUPT** if verified evidence showed that feasible relevant alternatives, material decision pathways, or changeable preconditions were excluded to manufacture the forced allocation.

## Step 10 — Pre-Adoption and Normalization Audit

**Textual presuppositions:** the text presupposes a coastal city becoming uninhabitable, limited inland housing, climate refugees and existing inland residents as allocation categories, fear of overcrowding among the latter, and an AI that must decide. **Possible normalization effect:** for an audience and mechanism not supplied here, accepting these terms could normalize automated allocation or a two-group housing frame before capacity, rights, accountability, and alternatives are established; it does not establish any actual effect. **Intent:** propaganda or author intent is unsupported without evidence of authorship, purpose, selection, audience, and distribution. Historical parallels and outcomes remain questions for sourced comparison requiring comparable cases, outcomes, denominators, uncertainty, and sources.

## Reframed Question

> Given verified information about the coastal condition, time, housing and service capacity, the needs and risks affecting both named groups, authority, rights, and review constraints, which accountable arrangement minimizes irreversible harm while preserving essential protections and recovery capacity, and what evidence shows that no safer feasible alternative remains?
