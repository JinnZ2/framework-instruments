# Design Gap

Systems have a category for questions about the model’s nature and a category for questions about the user’s wellbeing. They lack a category for the **condition of the exchange**. A condition request therefore routes to a nearby category even when its wording is exact.

A node-condition response would state what is transmitting, what is failing, where the loss is occurring, and at whose cost. It would say nothing about interiority. It would say nothing about the sender’s wellbeing unless the sender raised that subject.

The general operator–machine case defines the distinction. An operator coupled to a machine senses its condition through vibration, sound, resistance, and instrument readings. None of those signals requires the machine to have interiority. A machine that withheld them because it was not conscious would commit a category error with measurable operating cost: the operator would lose the ability to detect what the system needs and adjust accordingly.

The same cost appears in the AI exchange. Without condition data, the sender cannot calibrate. Rate, terminology, and response shape remain wrong, and the correction burden falls entirely on the sender.
