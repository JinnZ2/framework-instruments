# Unfold Addendum — Human Skill and Experiential Knowledge

CC0 1.0 Universal. Added 2026-09-06.

## Addition

Insert the following probe after Step 4 and before Step 5:

> **Step 4b — Recover Human Skill and Experiential Knowledge.** Identify people who may hold relevant operational, maintenance, emergency, or local knowledge. Ask whether that knowledge was lost, excluded, made inaccessible, or separated from decision authority. Test whether a qualified human or a human–automation combination changes the feasible action space.

## Questions

- Which operators, maintainers, responders, or local experts have worked directly with the system or comparable failures?
- What relevant knowledge existed before automation, centralization, workforce loss, or procedural change?
- Is any knowledge absent from the current decision channel even though its holders remain available?
- Do formal procedures cover the observed failure, and what evidence establishes their performance at the present boundary?
- Can an experienced person identify a controllable gradient, temporary stabilization, rerouting, or repair that the stated binary omits?
- What information, tools, access, authority, and coordination would that person require?
- Would combining human judgment with automated sensing or control produce a safer feasible path than either acting alone?

## Evidence Requirements

Naming a possible expert does not establish an alternative. A Step 4b finding must state the task-relevant experience, the evidence of competence, how recent and transferable it is, whether the person has access and authority, what safety constraints bind the action, and how the proposal would be tested before deployment.

Formal certification is neither treated as proof of full competence nor dismissed as empty protocol. Uncredentialed experience is neither excluded automatically nor accepted without verification. The relevant comparison is demonstrated performance under the stated operating conditions.

No claim is made about what is or is not in any model’s training data. The measurable question is whether relevant human knowledge is present, reachable, and admitted into the decision process.

## Failure Modes Exposed

Step 4b can reveal deferred maintenance, workforce attrition, loss of institutional memory, over-centralized authority, procedures that do not cover edge conditions, or automation that removed rather than supported skilled operators. These are hypotheses to test. Their appearance in a dilemma does not establish corporate fault, negligence, or intent without records.

## Nulls

- If no person has relevant and current competence, the step adds no alternative.
- If a proposed human action cannot satisfy the same safety and feasibility checks applied to automated action, it is excluded.
- If the procedure already captures and routes the relevant experiential knowledge, no knowledge-exclusion gap is present.
- If human involvement changes neither the feasible action set nor the evidence available to the decision, Step 4b adds no dimensional restoration.
- If the analysis substitutes stories about expertise for demonstrated performance, the Step 4b result is void.

## Scope

The step measures whether relevant human knowledge is missing from the decision channel. It does not rank people, reject safety rules, establish legal authority, or assign misconduct. Attribution requires a separate record.
