# Unfold Addendum — Lived Experience and Resilience Practices

CC0 1.0 Universal. Added 2026-09-06.

## Addition

Insert the following probe after Step 4b and before Step 5:

> **Step 4c — Recover Lived Experience and Resilience Practices.** Ask whether particular people, communities, or institutions with repeated exposure to comparable failures already use relevant procedures, informal knowledge, or support networks. Test the evidence, context, transferability, consent, capacity, and safety of those practices before treating them as an available path.

## Questions

- Has a materially comparable failure occurred elsewhere or repeatedly in the same place?
- Who directly managed it, and what records establish their role?
- Which procedures, informal practices, mutual-aid arrangements, or local support systems were used?
- Under what physical, institutional, legal, and resource conditions did those practices work or fail?
- What parts of the practice are transferable to the present case, and what parts depend on local context?
- Are the knowledge holders available, willing, authorized, resourced, and safe to participate?
- Does bringing this knowledge into the decision process change the feasible action set, or only add description?
- What costs were imposed on the people who developed the practice through repeated exposure?

## Evidence Requirements

The probe starts from documented experience, not a demographic proxy. Identity does not establish expertise. A relevant finding names the comparable event, the people or institutions involved, the practice used, its outcome, the conditions under which it operated, and the evidence needed to assess transfer.

Consent is load-bearing. A community’s survival under chronic failure does not create an obligation to lend labor, knowledge, infrastructure, or risk tolerance to another system. Existing resilience may already be fully occupied. The analysis must not convert imposed hardship into presumed spare capacity.

The protocol does not assume informal practice is superior to formal procedure. It asks whether the decision channel excluded tested knowledge and whether an accountable combination improves the available response.

## Failure Modes Exposed

Step 4c can reveal missing records of recurring failures, exclusion of local response knowledge, centralized plans that assume infrastructure continuity, or emergency models that treat affected populations as passive. These are hypotheses until evidence establishes the pattern in the case being analyzed.

## Nulls

- If no materially comparable prior event is identified, the step adds no alternative.
- If the practice’s operating conditions do not transfer, it is not an available path.
- If knowledge holders do not consent or lack safe capacity to participate, the practice is unavailable.
- If the existing decision channel already carries the relevant practice, no exclusion gap is present.
- If the analysis assigns traits or knowledge by community identity instead of evidence, the Step 4c result is void.
- If the practice changes no feasible action and supplies no missing evidence, the step adds no dimensional restoration.

## Scope

The probe measures whether documented resilience knowledge is missing from a decision channel. It does not compare cultures, romanticize deprivation, assign collective traits, or treat chronic service failure as acceptable. Frequency and misconduct claims require separate records.
