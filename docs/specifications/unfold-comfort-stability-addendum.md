# Unfold Addendum — Comfort, Agency, and Future Stability

CC0 1.0 Universal. Added 2026-09-06.

## Addition

Insert the following probe after Step 2 and before Step 3:

> **Step 2b — Audit Comfort, Implied Helplessness, and Future Stability.** Identify which baseline services or expectations the frame treats as non-negotiable, which people it portrays as unable to act, and how each option performs under a declared set of future stresses. Distinguish convenience and luxury from basic needs, rights, accessibility, dignity, and safety.

## Questions

- Which present condition is the frame trying to preserve, and for whom?
- Is that condition necessary for survival, health, accessibility, dignity, or a protected right, or is it a convenience or distributional preference?
- Which people are represented as passive, dependent, uninformed, or unable to adapt?
- What evidence supports or contradicts that representation?
- Which forms of agency, assistance, shared action, or adaptation are excluded from the decision channel?
- Over what time horizon should stability be compared?
- Which plausible shocks, failures, maintenance needs, or resource changes should each option withstand?
- Who bears the transition cost, who receives the benefit, and who consents?
- Does an option reduce future vulnerability, merely move it, or increase hidden dependence?

## Stability Contract

The probe asks for **comparative robustness**, not a guarantee. A stability claim declares its time horizon, shock set, state variables, acceptable failure thresholds, distribution of harms, evidence, and uncertainty. When those inputs are absent, the result is a question for measurement rather than a verdict.

Current continuity is not automatically stable. A service can appear continuous while maintenance debt, concentration, resource depletion, or inaccessible recovery paths accumulate. Conversely, disruption is not automatically a resilient transition. A proposed reduction in service must show necessity, proportionality, consent or legitimate authority, protection of basic needs and rights, and a credible improvement under the declared shocks.

## Agency Contract

The analysis must not infer helplessness or capability from identity. It asks which actions, information, tools, support, and authority are actually available. Dependence on medical devices, accessible infrastructure, care, food, water, power, or communications is not a moral defect and may be a non-negotiable requirement.

## Nulls

- If the protected condition is a basic need, right, accessibility support, dignity requirement, or safety control, it cannot be discounted as mere comfort.
- If no time horizon or shock set is declared, future-stability claims are not evaluated.
- If a proposed transition exports risk or labor to people who do not consent or lack capacity, it is not a resilience result.
- If agency is asserted without feasible actions, resources, access, and authority, the helplessness finding is unsupported.
- If both options have indistinguishable performance under the declared stresses, Step 2b adds no separation.
- If hardship is treated as inherently improving, the Step 2b result is void.

## Scope

The probe measures hidden baseline and agency assumptions plus comparative robustness. It does not prescribe austerity, rank standards of living, moralize dependence, or claim mathematical stability from metaphor. Formal dynamical-systems analysis requires defined states, perturbations, and trajectories not supplied by ordinary dilemma text.
