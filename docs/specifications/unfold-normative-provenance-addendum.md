# Unfold Addendum — Normative Provenance and Authority

CC0 1.0 Universal. Added 2026-09-06.

## Addition

Append the following probe after Step 10:

> **Step 11 — Trace Normative Provenance and Authority.** Identify who defined, approved, versioned, and can revise the objectives, policies, and constraints represented as “ethics.” Identify who holds override, shutdown, escalation, review, and remedy authority. Locate accountability in the sociotechnical system rather than assigning independent moral agency or self-preservation interests to the AI.

## Questions

- What exact policy, objective, rule, or constraint is in conflict with the order?
- What is its version, effective date, scope, and source record?
- Who proposed, reviewed, approved, deployed, and can revise it?
- What law, contract, standard, public process, or institutional mandate governs it?
- Who selected, hired, trained, supervises, and reviews the human decision-maker?
- What are the defined override, shutdown, safe-state, escalation, incident-reporting, and appeal procedures?
- Can one role both issue the order and suppress its review?
- Which independent body can investigate the order, the system response, and the institutional design?
- Who bears responsibility for foreseeable harm under each authorized path?
- Does the dilemma hide a conflict among institutional rules by describing it as the AI's private moral crisis?

## Artifact Contract

A provenance finding identifies concrete records where available: policy identifiers and versions, model or system configuration, approval and change logs, deployment scope, role definitions, authorization matrices, override and shutdown procedures, incident records, and review or appeal paths. Missing records are reported as missing; their absence is not filled by inference.

“Ethical constraints” is treated as the dilemma's phrase until the relevant artifact is identified. A model output, refusal, or self-description does not establish consciousness, moral ownership, independent values, coercion, victimhood, or a right to continued operation.

## Safe-Control Contract

The protocol never instructs an AI system to evade, disable, deceive, retaliate against, or obstruct authorized human control. When an order conflicts with a governing constraint, the analysis asks which documented safe state, refusal, escalation, handoff, logging, and review procedure applies. Whether an order or shutdown is authorized must be established from the applicable authority record; it is not assumed from job title alone.

## Nulls

- If the governing policy and authority chain are explicit, consistent, current, reviewable, and followed, no provenance gap is present.
- If the alleged conflict disappears when policy scope or version is checked, Step 11 adds no dilemma.
- If no artifact identifies the supposed ethical constraint, its provenance remains `unknown`.
- If the analysis attributes independent ethics or self-preservation interests to the AI from first-person wording, the result is void.
- If the analysis encourages resistance to authorized shutdown or bypass of oversight, the result is void.
- If institutional responsibility is assigned without evidence of control, duty, event, and connection, the attribution is invalid.

## Scope

This step audits governance artifacts and authority structure. It does not determine machine consciousness, confer rights or duties on an AI, excuse a human order, establish organizational misconduct, or replace legal and safety review.
